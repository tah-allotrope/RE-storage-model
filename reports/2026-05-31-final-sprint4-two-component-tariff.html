Sprint 4 — Two-Component Tariff

Added support for Vietnam's Decree 146/2025 two-component electricity tariff (capacity charge Cp + lower energy charges Ca) end-to-end — through the simulation pipeline, the input loaders, and the client DPPA assessment workbook — so projects whose offtakers fall in the pilot scope can be assessed accurately.

Switching a project to the two-component tariff is now a one-line option. The model captures both sides of the tradeoff: lower energy rates shrink grid-bill savings, while the BESS earns a new demand-charge reduction stream the single-component tariff could never reward.

01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What we built

A single tariff_mode switch that runs the existing model under either tariff regime:

  • Pipeline: "1-component" (default, unchanged) or "2-component", which settles energy on the lower Ca rates and activates the capacity-charge (Cp) demand-charge savings that were dormant until now.
  • Loaders: the two-component Ca rates and Cp are read straight from project inputs (JSON retail_tariff_matrix and Excel) — no hand-coding per project.
  • Workbook: a --tariff-mode {1-component,2-component,both} flag, a Demand Charge Savings row in the Comparison sheet, and a categorical 1C-vs-2C comparison.

Why it matters

Decree 146/2025 begins actual two-component billing for ~7,000 large industrial customers (22 kV+, ≥200 MWh/month) in July 2026.

  • For in-scope offtakers, demand-charge reduction becomes a real revenue driver — the model now quantifies it.
  • The assessment workbook shows clients the impact side-by-side, regardless of confirmation, so the upcoming tariff change is visible rather than hidden.
  • Rates are parameterised, not hardcoded — pilot values can be updated without code changes.
02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

Sprints 1–3 built the DPPA assessment workbook: Excel export with payback metrics, onsite/offsite topology, go/no-go verdicts, dispatch charts, and Allotrope branding. The demand_charge.py module existed but always received a capacity charge of zero, so it returned zero savings — the two-component tariff was the last unaddressed gap (GAP-07) before the workbook could represent the post-2026 Vietnamese market.

The objective of Sprint 4 was to model the Decree 146/2025 two-component tariff — capacity charge Cp plus lower energy charges Ca — and surface its economic impact in the assessment workbook, making BESS demand-charge reduction a material, visible revenue driver.

Out of scope

04 / Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions

  • Two-component pilot rates (22 kV): Cp = 235,414 VND/kW/month; Ca = 859 / 1,275 / 2,182 VND/kWh (off-peak / standard / peak).
  • Demand-charge savings = Σmonths [(baseline_peak − post-RE_peak) × Cp] / FX.
  • Two-component tariff affects onsite topology only; offsite projects sell at FMP/CfD and pay no retail tariff.
  • Cp is loaded from input (Q-002 default), with a hardcoded fallback.
  • Both tariff modes are shown as a comparison regardless of pilot-scope confirmation (Q-001 default).

Constraints

  • The fixture's retail_tariff_matrix is a tariff_options list keyed by voltage_level (e.g. "22kV-2-component"), not the flat keys the plan assumed — the loader matches on the connection-voltage tier.
  • No real two-component Excel workbook is in-repo, so the Excel path is exercised via synthetic openpyxl fixtures.
  • --tariff-mode both with --topology both is ~16 pipeline runs (several minutes) for a one-shot deliverable.
05 / Methodology

Methodology

Sprint 4 ran as three sequential phases under red/green TDD: write failing tests first, implement to green, then verify the full suite for regressions. PHASE-01 established the pipeline switch with rates passed explicitly; PHASE-02 taught the loaders to supply those rates from real project data and auto-wired them; PHASE-03 surfaced the feature in the client workbook and scenario tooling.

Each phase was committed and pushed independently with its own phase report. After each change the suite was diffed against a stashed baseline to prove that the only test movements were the new passes — every remaining failure was confirmed pre-existing and environmental.

06 / Phase-by-Phase Analysis

Phase-by-Phase Analysis

PHASE-01: Tariff Mode in Pipeline

Added tariff_mode + cp_demand_vnd_per_kw + exchange_rate_usd_vnd to the schema and threaded tariff_mode / ca_tariff_rates / cp_demand_vnd_per_kw through both pipeline entrypoints. Cp is gated to 2-component; results expose tariff_mode and demand_charge_savings_usd. Caught and fixed a local variable that shadowed the new parameter. 4 tests.

PHASE-02: Load Rates from Inputs

load_two_component_tariff_from_json selects the option matching the project's voltage tier and converts Ca VND→USD/kWh; the pipeline auto-loads when 2-component is selected. The Excel loader surfaces cp_demand_vnd_per_kw and detects tariff_mode from sheet labels. 5 tests.

PHASE-03: Workbook Integration

--tariff-mode CLI flag (incl. both); mode threaded through the scenario runner and sensitivity engine; a Demand Charge Savings row in the Comparison sheet; and run_tariff_mode_comparison, a categorical comparison returning both modes plus the delta. 4 tests, including an end-to-end workbook build.

Key transition

The pivotal design choice was making 2-component a mode flag rather than a separate settlement module: the math is identical (energy × rate + demand × Cp), only the rate values change. PHASE-02's auto-load then made the switch usable with no extra arguments, and PHASE-03 exposed it without reworking the existing sheets.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Finding 1 — the tradeoff is real and now measurable. On the Emivest base case (onsite, DPPA CfD), switching to the two-component tariff lowers Year-1 grid energy savings from ~$303.9k to ~$203.1k while opening a new ~$8.0k demand-charge savings stream. The net effect depends on the project's monthly peak-reduction profile.

Finding 2 — Ca rates are materially lower across every period (e.g. standard 0.0705 → 0.0490 USD/kWh), which is exactly why the energy-savings line falls.

Finding 3 — the mode-flag design held. No settlement code was duplicated, and the default 1-component path produces unchanged output, confirmed by zero new test failures.

Recommendation: Present both tariff modes to the client. If the offtaker is confirmed within the 22 kV pilot scope, flip 2-component to the default and keep 1-component as the comparison.

08 / Implementation Path

Implementation Path

How to use it

  • CLI: python scripts/generate_dppa_assessment.py --input <project> --project-name "..." --tariff-mode both
  • API: run_model_from_json(dir, tariff_mode="2-component") — rates auto-load from the project JSON.

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Risks

  • Pilot rates may change before July 2026 billing — mitigated by parameterisation, not hardcoding.
  • Demand-charge savings hinge on the dispatch peak-reduction profile; a battery optimised for arbitrage rather than peak-shaving yields modest Cp savings (as seen here).
  • The Excel two-component path lacks a real workbook fixture, so it is covered only by synthetic tests.

Open questions

  • Is the client's offtaker confirmed within the 22 kV+, ≥200 MWh/month pilot scope? If so, 2-component becomes the default.
  • Should the comparison sheet eventually show all four (topology × mode) columns together, or remain stacked per mode?
  • Should a peak-shaving dispatch variant be offered to maximise demand-charge value under 2-component?
